# ⚡ PHYSICS

# 1. LIGHT — REFLECTION AND REFRACTION

## 🔦 Reflection of Light

**Reflection** is the bouncing back of light into the same medium after striking a surface.

### Laws of Reflection

1. The angle of incidence = angle of reflection.
    

**∠i = ∠r**

2. The incident ray, reflected ray and normal all lie in the same plane.
    

---

## 🔹 Important Terms

**Incident ray:** Ray of light falling on the surface.

**Reflected ray:** Ray that bounces back.

**Normal:** Perpendicular line drawn at the point of incidence.

**Angle of incidence (i):** Angle between incident ray and normal.

**Angle of reflection (r):** Angle between reflected ray and normal.

---

# 🪞 Spherical Mirrors

There are two types:

### Concave Mirror

Reflecting surface curves inward.

**Uses:**

- Shaving/makeup mirrors
    
- Dentist mirrors
    
- Headlights
    
- Solar furnaces
    

### Convex Mirror

Reflecting surface curves outward.

**Uses:**

- Rear-view mirrors of vehicles
    
- Security mirrors
    

### ⭐ Why convex mirrors are used as rear-view mirrors?

Because they provide:

- A wider field of view
    
- Upright images
    
- Smaller images
    

---

# 📌 Important Parts of a Spherical Mirror

**Pole (P):** Centre of the reflecting surface.

**Centre of Curvature (C):** Centre of the sphere of which the mirror is a part.

**Radius of Curvature (R):** Distance between P and C.

**Principal Axis:** Straight line passing through P and C.

**Principal Focus (F):** Point where parallel rays meet or appear to meet after reflection.

### Important relation:

**R = 2f**

or

**f = R/2**

---

# 📐 Mirror Formula

### ⭐

**1/f = 1/v + 1/u**

Where:

- **f** = focal length
    
- **v** = image distance
    
- **u** = object distance
    

---

# 🔢 Magnification

### Mirror:

**m = hᵢ/hₒ = -v/u**

Where:

- hᵢ = image height
    
- hₒ = object height
    

### Interpretation

**m > 1** → enlarged image

**m < 1** → diminished image

**m = 1** → same size

---

# 🔄 Sign Convention

Using the Cartesian sign convention:

- All distances are measured from the pole.
    
- Distances measured in the direction of incident light are positive.
    
- Distances measured opposite to incident light are negative.
    
- Heights above principal axis are positive.
    
- Heights below principal axis are negative.
    

---

# 💎 Refraction of Light

**Refraction** is the bending of light when it passes obliquely from one transparent medium to another.

This happens because the **speed of light changes**.

### Example:

Air → Glass

Light bends **towards the normal**.

Glass → Air

Light bends **away from the normal**.

---

# 📐 Laws of Refraction

### First Law

The incident ray, refracted ray and normal lie in the same plane.

### Second Law — Snell's Law

**sin i / sin r = constant**

This constant is called the **refractive index**.

---

# 🔹 Refractive Index

**n = c/v**

Where:

- c = speed of light in vacuum
    
- v = speed of light in the medium
    

Higher refractive index → lower speed of light.

---

# 🔍 Lens

Two main types:

### Convex Lens

Thicker at the centre and thinner at the edges.

It is a **converging lens**.

### Concave Lens

Thinner at the centre and thicker at the edges.

It is a **diverging lens**.

---

# 📐 Lens Formula

### ⭐

**1/f = 1/v - 1/u**

---

# 🔢 Magnification of Lens

**m = hᵢ/hₒ = v/u**

---

# ⭐ POWER OF LENS

Power tells us the ability of a lens to converge or diverge light.

### Formula:

**P = 1/f**

Here **f must be in metres**.

### Unit:

**Dioptre (D)**

### Sign:

Convex lens → **positive power**

Concave lens → **negative power**

---

**Refraction → bending of light**

**Convex mirror → diverging**

**Convex lens → converging**

**Concave lens → diverging**

**Mirror formula → 1/f = 1/v + 1/u**

**Lens formula → 1/f = 1/v − 1/u**

**Lens power → P = 1/f**

# 2. THE HUMAN EYE AND THE COLOURFUL WORLD

# 👁️ Human Eye

The human eye is a natural optical instrument that enables us to see objects.

## Main Parts

### Cornea

Transparent front surface of the eye.

It allows light to enter.

### Iris

Coloured part of the eye.

It controls the size of the pupil.

### Pupil

Opening through which light enters the eye.

### Eye Lens

Focuses light on the retina.

### Retina

Light-sensitive screen where the image is formed.

### Optic Nerve

Carries visual information from the retina to the brain.

---

# 🔄 Accommodation

The ability of the eye lens to change its focal length to focus on objects at different distances is called **accommodation**.

The **ciliary muscles** help change the shape of the lens.

---

# 👁️ Near Point and Far Point

For a normal adult eye:

### Near Point

**25 cm**

### Far Point

**Infinity (∞)**

---

# 👓 DEFECTS OF VISION

## 1. Myopia — Short-Sightedness

A person can see nearby objects clearly but cannot see distant objects clearly.

### Cause

Image forms **in front of the retina**.

### Correction

Uses a **concave lens**.

---

## 2. Hypermetropia — Long-Sightedness

A person can see distant objects clearly but has difficulty seeing nearby objects.

### Cause

Image forms **behind the retina**.

### Correction

Uses a **convex lens**.

---

## 3. Presbyopia

Occurs mainly due to ageing.

The eye loses its ability to accommodate properly.

### Correction

Usually corrected using suitable converging/diverging lenses depending on the defect, often **bifocal or progressive lenses** when both near and distant vision need correction.

---

# 🌈 Dispersion of Light

The splitting of white light into its seven constituent colours is called **dispersion**.

The colours are:

**VIBGYOR**

V — Violet  
I — Indigo  
B — Blue  
G — Green  
Y — Yellow  
O — Orange  
R — Red

A **prism** can produce dispersion.

---

# 🌈 Atmospheric Refraction

Atmospheric refraction occurs because light travels through layers of air having different densities.

### Examples:

- Twinkling of stars
    
- Advanced sunrise
    
- Delayed sunset
    

---

# ⭐ Why do stars twinkle?

Stars are very far away and appear as point sources.

Their light passes through changing layers of Earth's atmosphere, causing continuous changes in refraction.

Therefore, their apparent brightness and position fluctuate.

---

# 🔵 Why is the sky blue?

The atmosphere scatters shorter wavelengths of visible light more strongly.

Blue light is scattered much more than red light.

Therefore, the sky appears blue.

---

# 🌅 Why does the Sun appear reddish at sunrise and sunset?

Sunlight travels a longer distance through the atmosphere.

Most of the shorter wavelengths are scattered away.

More red/orange light reaches our eyes.

Therefore, the Sun appears reddish.

---

# 3. ELECTRICITY

# ⚡ Electric Current

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge.

### Formula:

**I = Q/t**

Where:

- I = current
    
- Q = charge
    
- t = time
    

### SI Unit:

**Ampere (A)**

---

# 🔋 Potential Difference

Potential difference is the work done to move a unit charge between two points.

### Formula:

**V = W/Q**

Unit = **Volt (V)**

---

# 🧱 Resistance

Resistance is the opposition offered by a conductor to the flow of electric current.

### Ohm's Law

At constant temperature:

**V ∝ I**

Therefore:

### ⭐

**V = IR**

---

# 🔹 Resistance of a Conductor

**R = ρl/A**

Where:

- R = resistance
    
- ρ = resistivity
    
- l = length
    
- A = cross-sectional area
    

### Resistance increases when:

- Length increases
    
- Area decreases
    

---

# 🔗 RESISTORS IN SERIES

For resistors connected in series:

### ⭐

**R = R₁ + R₂ + R₃**

Same current flows through every resistor.

Total voltage is divided among the resistors.

---

# 🔗 RESISTORS IN PARALLEL

### ⭐

**1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃**

Same potential difference exists across each resistor.

Current divides among different branches.

---

# 🔥 Heating Effect of Electric Current

When current passes through a resistor, electrical energy is converted into heat.

### Joule's Law:

**H = I²Rt**

Also:

**H = VIt**

and

**H = V²t/R**

---

# 💡 Electric Power

Electric power is the rate at which electrical energy is consumed.

### Formula:

**P = VI**

Using Ohm's law:

**P = I²R**

**P = V²/R**

### Unit:

**Watt (W)**

---

# ⚡ Electrical Energy

### Formula:

**E = Pt**

Commercial unit:

### **1 kilowatt-hour (kWh)**

**1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J**

---

# 🧠 ELECTRICITY FORMULA BOX

**I = Q/t**

**V = W/Q**

**V = IR**

**R = ρl/A**

**Series: R = R₁ + R₂ + R₃**

**Parallel: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃**

**H = I²Rt**

**P = VI**

**P = I²R**

**P = V²/R**

**E = Pt**

**1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J**

# 🍎 NUTRITION

Nutrition is the process by which organisms obtain and use food.

---

# 🌱 AUTOTROPHIC NUTRITION

Organisms that prepare their own food are called **autotrophs**.

Example:

**Green plants**

They prepare food through **photosynthesis**.

---

# ☀️ PHOTOSYNTHESIS

Plants use:

- Carbon dioxide
    
- Water
    
- Sunlight
    
- Chlorophyll
    

to produce glucose and oxygen.

### Equation:

**6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂**

Sunlight and chlorophyll are required.

---

# 🧪 HETEROTROPHIC NUTRITION

Organisms that depend on other organisms for food are called **heterotrophs**.

Types include:

### Holozoic

Organism takes in solid/liquid food.

Example: Humans

### Saprophytic

Organism obtains nutrients from dead and decaying matter.

Example: Fungi

### Parasitic

Organism obtains food from a living host.

Example: Cuscuta

---

# 👨‍🔬 HUMAN DIGESTIVE SYSTEM

## 1. Mouth

Food is chewed and mixed with saliva.

### Saliva contains:

**Salivary amylase**

It begins the digestion of starch.

---

## 2. Oesophagus

Carries food from mouth to stomach by **peristalsis**.

---

## 3. Stomach

Produces:

- Hydrochloric acid
    
- Pepsin
    
- Mucus
    

### Hydrochloric acid

Creates an acidic medium and helps digestion.

### Pepsin

Digests proteins.

### Mucus

Protects the stomach lining.

---

## 4. Small Intestine

Major site of digestion and absorption.

### Bile

Produced by the **liver**.

It helps in the digestion of fats by **emulsification**.

### Pancreatic juice

Produced by the pancreas.

Contains enzymes that digest carbohydrates, proteins and fats.

### Villi

Finger-like projections in the small intestine.

They increase the surface area for absorption.

---

## 5. Large Intestine

Absorbs excess water.

---

## 6. Rectum and Anus

Store and eliminate undigested waste.

---

# 🫁 RESPIRATION

Respiration is the process of obtaining energy from food.

---

# 🔥 AEROBIC RESPIRATION

Occurs in the presence of oxygen.

### Equation:

**Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy**

It releases a large amount of energy.

---

# 🦠 ANAEROBIC RESPIRATION

Occurs without oxygen.

### In yeast:

**Glucose → Ethanol + CO₂ + Energy**

### In muscles during vigorous exercise:

**Glucose → Lactic acid + Energy**

Lactic acid accumulation can cause muscle fatigue.

---

# ⚡ ATP

ATP stands for:

**Adenosine Triphosphate**

ATP is the energy currency of the cell.

Energy released during respiration is used to produce ATP.

---

The circulatory system consists mainly of:

- Blood

The human heart has **four chambers**:

- Right atrium

---

# 🔄 DOUBLE CIRCULATION

In humans, blood passes through the heart **twice during one complete cycle**.

## Pulmonary Circulation

Heart → Lungs → Heart

Carries blood between the heart and lungs.

## Systemic Circulation

Heart → Body → Heart

Carries oxygenated blood to the body.

---

# 🩸 BLOOD

Blood consists of:

### RBCs

Contain haemoglobin and transport oxygen.

### WBCs

Help defend the body against infections.

### Platelets

Help in blood clotting.

### Plasma

Liquid part of blood that transports various substances.

---

# 🩸 BLOOD VESSELS

### Arteries

Carry blood **away from the heart**.

Usually carry oxygenated blood.

**Exception:** Pulmonary artery.

### Veins

Carry blood **towards the heart**.

Usually carry deoxygenated blood.

**Exception:** Pulmonary vein.

### Capillaries

Very thin vessels where exchange of materials occurs between blood and tissues.

# 🧪 CHEMISTRY

# 5. CHEMICAL REACTIONS AND EQUATIONS

A **chemical reaction** is a process in which one or more substances are converted into new substances.

### Example:

**2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO**

Magnesium + Oxygen → Magnesium oxide

---

# 🔹 Signs of a Chemical Reaction

A chemical reaction may involve:

- Change in colour
    
- Change in temperature
    
- Formation of gas
    
- Formation of precipitate
    
- Change in state
    

---

# ⚖️ BALANCED CHEMICAL EQUATION

A chemical equation is balanced when the number of atoms of each element is equal on both sides.

### Example:

Unbalanced:

**H₂ + O₂ → H₂O**

Balanced:

### ⭐

**2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O**

---

# 🔥 TYPES OF CHEMICAL REACTIONS

## 1. Combination Reaction

Two or more substances combine to form one product.

**CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂**

---

## 2. Decomposition Reaction

One compound breaks into two or more simpler substances.

### Thermal decomposition:

**CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂**

### Electrolytic decomposition:

**2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂**

### Photolytic decomposition:

**2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl₂**

---

# 3. Displacement Reaction

A more reactive element displaces a less reactive element.

**Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu**

Zinc displaces copper.

---

# 4. Double Displacement Reaction

Two compounds exchange ions.

**Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄ + 2NaCl**

BaSO₄ forms a white precipitate.

---

# ⚡ OXIDATION AND REDUCTION

### Oxidation

Addition of oxygen or removal of hydrogen.

Example:

**2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO**

Copper is oxidised.

### Reduction

Removal of oxygen or addition of hydrogen.

---

# 🔄 REDOX REACTION

A reaction in which oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously.

Example:

**CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O**

CuO is reduced.

H₂ is oxidised.

---

# 🧲 CORROSION

Corrosion is the gradual destruction of metals due to reactions with their environment.

Example:

**Rusting of iron**

Iron reacts with oxygen and moisture to form rust.

---

# 🛡️ RANCIDITY

Rancidity is the oxidation of fats and oils, causing unpleasant smell and taste.

### Prevention:

- Refrigeration
    
- Airtight containers
    
- Antioxidants
    
- Nitrogen flushing
    

---

# 🧪 6. ACIDS, BASES AND SALTS

# 🍋 ACIDS

Acids produce **H⁺ ions** in aqueous solution.

Examples:

- Hydrochloric acid — HCl
    
- Sulphuric acid — H₂SO₄
    
- Nitric acid — HNO₃
    
- Acetic acid — CH₃COOH
    

### Properties

- Sour taste
    
- Turn blue litmus red
    
- React with some metals
    
- React with bases
    

---

# 🧼 BASES

Bases produce **OH⁻ ions** in aqueous solution.

Examples:

- Sodium hydroxide — NaOH
    
- Calcium hydroxide — Ca(OH)₂
    
- Potassium hydroxide — KOH
    

### Properties

- Bitter taste
    
- Soapy feel
    
- Turn red litmus blue
    
- React with acids
    

---

# 🌈 INDICATORS

Indicators help identify whether a substance is acidic or basic.

### Litmus

Acid → Blue litmus becomes **red**

Base → Red litmus becomes **blue**

### Phenolphthalein

Acid → Colourless

Base → Pink

### Methyl orange

Acid → Red

Base → Yellow

---

# 📊 pH SCALE

The pH scale measures how acidic or basic a solution is.

Range:

### **0 → 14**

**pH < 7 → Acidic**

**pH = 7 → Neutral**

**pH > 7 → Basic**

Lower pH → stronger acidity.

Higher pH → stronger basicity.

---

# ⚗️ REACTIONS OF ACIDS

## Acid + Metal

→ Salt + Hydrogen

Example:

**Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂**

---

## Acid + Metal Carbonate

→ Salt + Water + Carbon dioxide

**Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂**

---

## Acid + Base

→ Salt + Water

This is called **neutralisation**.

**HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O**

---

# 🧂 SALTS

Salts are ionic compounds generally formed when acids react with bases.

Examples:

- Sodium chloride — NaCl
    
- Sodium bicarbonate — NaHCO₃
    
- Bleaching powder — CaOCl₂
    
- Baking soda — NaHCO₃
    
- Washing soda — Na₂CO₃·10H₂O
    

---

# 🥄 BAKING SODA

Chemical name:

**Sodium hydrogen carbonate**

Formula:

**NaHCO₃**

### Uses:

- Baking
    
- Antacid
    
- Fire extinguishers

**2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂**

---

# 🧺 WASHING SODA

Chemical name:

**Sodium carbonate decahydrate**

Formula:

**Na₂CO₃·10H₂O**

### Uses:

- Cleaning agent
    
- Removing permanent hardness of water
    
- Glass, soap and paper industries
    

---

# 🧼 BLEACHING POWDER

Formula:

**CaOCl₂**

### Uses:

- Disinfecting drinking water
    
- Bleaching cotton and linen
    
- Bleaching wood pulp
    

---

# 🧱 PLASTER OF PARIS

Formula:

**CaSO₄·½H₂O**

It is prepared by heating gypsum.

### Uses:

- Making casts for fractured bones
    
- Making statues
    
- Decorative materials
    

---

# 💧 WATER OF CRYSTALLISATION

The fixed number of water molecules present in one formula unit of a salt is called water of crystallisation.

Example:

**CuSO₄·5H₂O**

Copper sulphate crystals contain five molecules of water of crystallisation.
