# 📜 HISTORY

# Chapter 1 — The Rise of Nationalism in Europe

## French Revolution and Nationalism

The **French Revolution (1789)** introduced ideas of:

- Liberty
- Equality
- Fraternity
- Democracy
- National sovereignty

It transferred sovereignty from the monarchy to the citizens.

### Changes introduced by the French Revolution

- Abolition of privileges based on birth.
- Establishment of equality before law.
- Introduction of a common national identity.
- Creation of a centralised administrative system.
- Introduction of common weights and measures.
- French became a common language.

---

## Napoleon and the Napoleonic Code

**Napoleonic Code (1804)** is also known as the **Civil Code of 1804**.

### Important features

- Equality before the law.
- Abolition of privileges based on birth.
- Secured the right to property.
- Simplified administrative divisions.
- Abolished feudal systems in many areas.

> [!important]
> Napoleon introduced many reforms, but his rule was also authoritarian and involved military expansion.

---

## Congress of Vienna — 1815

The **Congress of Vienna** was hosted by **Duke Metternich of Austria**.

### Main objectives

- Restore monarchies that had been overthrown by Napoleon.
- Establish a conservative order in Europe.
- Redraw European boundaries.
- Prevent future French expansion.

---

## Liberalism

Liberalism stood for:

- Freedom for individuals.
- Equality before law.
- Representative government.
- Freedom of markets.
- End of privileges based on birth.

> Liberalism did **not initially mean universal adult franchise**. Voting rights were often restricted to property-owning men.

---

## Nationalism

**Nationalism** is the feeling of belonging to a common nation based on shared identity, culture, history and political aspirations.

---

## Giuseppe Mazzini

- Italian revolutionary.
- Founded **Young Italy**.
- Believed Italy should become a unified republic.
- Played an important role in spreading nationalist ideas.

---

## Unification of Germany

Germany was divided into many states.

### Important figure

**Otto von Bismarck**

He used:

- The Prussian army
- Diplomacy
- Three wars

### Major wars

1. War with Denmark
2. Austro-Prussian War
3. Franco-Prussian War

Germany was unified in **1871**.

**King Wilhelm I of Prussia** was proclaimed German Emperor.

---

## Unification of Italy

Important figures:

### Giuseppe Mazzini
Spread the idea of a united Italy.

### Count Cavour
- Chief Minister of Sardinia-Piedmont.
- Used diplomacy and alliances.

### Giuseppe Garibaldi
- Led armed volunteers.
- Helped conquer territories in southern Italy.

### Victor Emmanuel II
Became the king of unified Italy.

---

## Balkan Nationalism

The Balkans included areas inhabited by different ethnic groups.

### Causes of tension

- Decline of the Ottoman Empire.
- Rise of nationalism.
- Competition among European powers.
- Conflicting claims of different ethnic groups.

The Balkan region became a major source of tension in Europe.

# Chapter 2 — Nationalism in India

## First World War and India

The First World War created:

- Increased defence expenditure.
- Higher taxes.
- Rising prices.
- Forced recruitment.
- Crop failures.
- Food shortages.

These conditions increased dissatisfaction among Indians.

---

## Satyagraha

**Mahatma Gandhi** introduced the idea of **Satyagraha**.

### Meaning

Satyagraha means insisting on truth and opposing injustice through **non-violent resistance**.

### Principles

- Truth
- Non-violence
- Peaceful resistance

---

## Gandhi's Early Satyagraha Movements

### Champaran — 1917

Movement against the **indigo plantation system**.

### Kheda — 1918

Peasants demanded relaxation in revenue collection because of crop failure.

### Ahmedabad — 1918

Gandhi supported cotton mill workers in their dispute for better working conditions.

---

## Rowlatt Act — 1919

The Rowlatt Act allowed the British government to:

- Arrest political prisoners without trial.
- Suppress political activities.

Gandhi organised a nationwide **Satyagraha against the Act**.

---

## Jallianwala Bagh Massacre

**13 April 1919**

A large crowd gathered at Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar.

General **Dyer** ordered troops to fire on the crowd.

The incident created widespread anger against British rule.

---

## Non-Cooperation Movement

Launched in **1920** under Gandhi's leadership.

### Methods

- Boycott government schools.
- Boycott colleges.
- Boycott courts.
- Boycott foreign cloth.
- Surrender government titles.
- Promote Swadeshi.

### Withdrawal

The movement was withdrawn in **1922** after the **Chauri Chaura incident**, where protesters turned violent and a police station was set on fire.

---

## Civil Disobedience Movement

Started in **1930**.

### Salt March

Gandhi marched from **Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi**.

He broke the salt law at Dandi.

### Meaning

Civil disobedience means openly refusing to obey unjust laws.

---

## Participation of Different Groups

### Rich Peasants

Wanted reduction in land revenue.

### Poor Peasants

Wanted relief from high rents and debts.

### Business Classes

Wanted protection from foreign imports.

### Women

Participated in:

- Processions
- Picketing
- Salt Satyagraha

### Industrial Workers

Participation was limited and varied across regions.

# 🌍 GEOGRAPHY

# Chapter 1 — Resources and Development

## Resource

Anything that has:

- Utility
- Economic value
- Technological feasibility
- Cultural acceptability

can be considered a resource.

---

## Types of Resources

### Based on Origin

**Biotic**
- Obtained from living organisms.
- Example: forests, animals.

**Abiotic**
- Obtained from non-living things.
- Example: rocks, metals.

---

### Based on Exhaustibility

**Renewable Resources**

Can be renewed or reproduced.

Examples:

- Solar energy
- Wind energy
- Water
- Forests

**Non-renewable Resources**

Available in limited quantities and take millions of years to form.

Examples:

- Coal
- Petroleum
- Natural gas

---

### Based on Ownership

- Individual resources
- Community resources
- National resources
- International resources

---

### Based on Status of Development

**Potential Resources**

Resources found in a region but not fully used.

**Developed Resources**

Resources whose quantity and quality have been surveyed and are being used.

**Stock**

Materials that have the potential to satisfy human needs but cannot currently be used due to lack of appropriate technology.

**Reserves**

Resources that can be used to meet future requirements.

---

# Resource Planning

**Resource planning** means identifying, surveying, mapping and developing resources according to their availability and requirements.

### Steps

1. Identification and inventory of resources.
2. Planning structure with appropriate technology, skills and institutions.
3. Matching resource development plans with overall national development plans.

---

# Land Resources

Land is used for:

- Forests
- Agriculture
- Houses
- Roads
- Industries
- Pastures

---

# Land Degradation

**Land degradation** means decline in the quality and productivity of land.

### Causes

- Deforestation
- Overgrazing
- Mining
- Over-irrigation
- Industrial waste

### Measures

- Afforestation
- Controlled grazing
- Proper irrigation
- Treatment of industrial waste
- Planting shelter belts

---

# Soil

Soil is an important natural resource for agriculture.

## Major Soil Types

### Alluvial Soil

Found mainly in the northern plains and river valleys.

Important for:

- Rice
- Wheat
- Sugarcane
- Pulses

### Black Soil

Also called **Regur soil**.

Important for:

- Cotton

Characteristics:

- Clayey
- Holds moisture well

### Red and Yellow Soil

Found mainly in parts of eastern and southern India.

### Laterite Soil

Found in areas of high temperature and heavy rainfall.

Suitable for:

- Tea
- Coffee
- Cashew with proper manure and fertilisers

### Arid Soil

Found mainly in dry regions.

### Forest/Mountain Soil

Found in mountainous and forest regions.

---

# Soil Erosion

**Soil erosion** is the removal of the top fertile layer of soil by wind, water or other agents.

### Methods of Soil Conservation

- Contour ploughing
- Terrace farming
- Strip cropping
- Shelter belts
- Afforestation

---

# Chapter 2 — Forest and Wildlife Resources

## Biodiversity

**Biodiversity** refers to the variety of living organisms found in an area.

---

## Flora and Fauna

**Flora** → Plants of a particular region.

**Fauna** → Animals of a particular region.

---

## Categories of Species

### Normal Species

Population level considered normal for survival.

### Endangered Species

Species facing a very high risk of extinction.

### Vulnerable Species

Species whose population is declining and may become endangered.

### Rare Species

Species with small populations and limited distribution.

### Endemic Species

Species found only in a particular geographical area.

### Extinct Species

Species that are no longer found.

---

## Causes of Depletion of Forests and Wildlife

- Agricultural expansion
- Commercial exploitation
- Mining
- Development projects
- Hunting
- Pollution
- Over-exploitation of resources

---

## Conservation

Conservation means protection and careful management of natural resources.

### Importance

- Maintains ecological balance.
- Protects biodiversity.
- Prevents extinction.
- Maintains food chains.
- Supports livelihoods.

---

## Important Conservation Movements

### Chipko Movement

People resisted the cutting of forests by hugging trees.

### Joint Forest Management

Local communities participate in protecting and managing forests.

---

# Chapter 3 — Water Resources

## Water Scarcity

**Water scarcity** means shortage of sufficient usable water.

### Causes

- Population growth
- Urbanisation
- Industrialisation
- Intensive agriculture
- Over-exploitation
- Unequal distribution of rainfall

---

# Multipurpose River Valley Projects

These projects are built for multiple purposes.

### Uses

- Irrigation
- Electricity generation
- Flood control
- Water supply
- Navigation
- Recreation

### Examples

- Bhakra Nangal
- Tehri
- Sardar Sarovar

---

## Problems of Large Dams

- Displacement of people.
- Submergence of forests.
- Loss of wildlife habitats.
- Changes in river ecosystems.
- Conflicts over water sharing.

---

# Rainwater Harvesting

**Rainwater harvesting** means collecting and storing rainwater for future use or groundwater recharge.

### Benefits

- Conserves water.
- Recharges groundwater.
- Reduces dependence on groundwater.
- Helps during water shortages.

### Traditional Methods

Examples include:

- Khadins
- Tankas
- Johads
- Kuls

# 🏛️ CIVICS

# Chapter 1 — Power Sharing

## Power Sharing

**Power sharing** means distributing power among different organs, levels, groups or communities of government.

---

## Why is Power Sharing Desirable?

### Prudential Reason

Power sharing:

- Reduces conflicts.
- Reduces the possibility of violence.
- Promotes political stability.

### Moral Reason

Power sharing is the **spirit of democracy**.

People affected by decisions should have a role in making those decisions.

---

# Forms of Power Sharing

## 1. Among Different Organs

Power is shared among:

- Legislature
- Executive
- Judiciary

This is called **horizontal distribution of power**.

---

## 2. Among Different Levels

Power is shared between:

- Central/Union government
- State government
- Local government

This is called **vertical distribution of power**.

---

## 3. Among Social Groups

Power may be shared among different:

- Religious groups
- Linguistic groups
- Social groups

Example:

- Community government in Belgium.

---

## 4. Among Political Parties and Pressure Groups

Different political parties and pressure groups influence decision-making.

---

# Belgium

Belgium has different linguistic communities:

- Dutch-speaking
- French-speaking
- German-speaking

### Belgian Model

Belgium adopted power-sharing arrangements to avoid conflict.

Important features:

- Equal number of Dutch- and French-speaking ministers at the central government.
- State governments have important powers.
- Brussels has a separate government with equal representation.
- Community government deals with cultural, educational and language-related matters.

---

# Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka adopted **majoritarianism**.

The Sinhala majority gained political dominance.

### Majoritarian Measures

- Sinhala made the official language.
- Preference was given to Sinhala applicants in education and government jobs.
- Buddhism was given special protection.

These policies created dissatisfaction among Tamil communities and contributed to ethnic conflict.

---

# Chapter 2 — Federalism

## Federalism

**Federalism** is a system of government in which power is divided between two or more levels of government.

---

## Key Features

1. Two or more levels of government.
2. Same citizens are governed by different levels.
3. Constitutional division of powers.
4. Constitutional supremacy.
5. Courts settle disputes between levels.
6. Sources of revenue are divided among governments.

---

# Types of Federations

## Coming Together Federation

Independent states come together to form a larger unit.

Examples:

- USA
- Switzerland
- Australia

## Holding Together Federation

A large country divides power between the central government and states.

Examples:

- India
- Spain
- Belgium

---

# Federalism in India

India has:

- Union Government
- State Governments
- Local Governments

### Three Lists

#### Union List

Subjects of national importance.

Examples:

- Defence
- Foreign affairs
- Banking
- Currency

#### State List

Subjects mainly handled by state governments.

Examples:

- Police
- Agriculture
- Public health

#### Concurrent List

Both Union and State governments can make laws.

Examples:

- Education
- Forests
- Marriage

---

## Residuary Subjects

Subjects not included in any of the three lists are called **residuary subjects**.

In India, the Union Government has the power to legislate on them.

---

# Decentralisation

**Decentralisation** means transferring power from higher levels of government to lower levels.

### Local Government

#### Rural

- Gram Panchayat
- Panchayat Samiti
- Zilla Parishad

#### Urban

- Municipal Corporation
- Municipality

---

# Language Policy in India

India did not declare any one language as the national language.

Hindi was made the official language of the Union, while English continues to be used for official purposes.

The Constitution recognises multiple scheduled languages.

# 💰 ECONOMICS

# Chapter 1 — Development

## Development

Development refers to improvement in people's quality of life and achievement of desired goals.

Different people can have **different developmental goals**.

---

# Different Developmental Goals

For example:

### Landless Rural Labourer

May want:

- More days of work
- Better wages
- Better working conditions

### Rich Farmer

May want:

- Higher crop prices
- Cheap electricity
- Better irrigation

### Student

May want:

- Quality education
- Good career opportunities
- Security

> Development goals can sometimes conflict with each other.

---

# Income and Other Goals

Income is important, but people also want:

- Equal treatment
- Freedom
- Security
- Respect
- Good health
- Education
- Clean environment

Therefore, **income alone cannot measure development**.

---

# Per Capita Income

**Per capita income** is the average income of a person in a country.

### Formula

$$
\text{Per Capita Income}
=
\frac{\text{Total Income}}{\text{Total Population}}
$$

It is commonly used to compare average income between countries or states.

---

# Other Important Indicators

## Infant Mortality Rate (IMR)

Number of children who die before reaching one year of age, per 1,000 live births.

Lower IMR generally indicates better healthcare conditions.

---

## Literacy Rate

Percentage of people in a population who can read and write with understanding.

---

## Net Attendance Ratio

Percentage of children in the relevant age group attending school.

---

# Public Facilities

Facilities provided for the welfare of people include:

- Schools
- Hospitals
- Clean drinking water
- Sanitation
- Public transport
- Electricity

Public facilities improve people's quality of life.

---

# Human Development

Human development considers factors such as:

- Income
- Health
- Education
- Standard of living

The **Human Development Report** is published by the **UNDP**.

---

# Sustainable Development

**Sustainable development** means development that meets present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their needs.

### Ways to Promote Sustainable Development

- Use renewable resources.
- Reduce pollution.
- Conserve water.
- Protect forests.
- Use resources carefully.
- Reduce unnecessary consumption.

# ⭐ IMPORTANT DIFFERENCES

## Renewable vs Non-Renewable Resources

| Renewable | Non-Renewable |
|---|---|
| Can be renewed naturally | Limited stock |
| Can be replenished | Take a very long time to form |
| Solar, wind, water | Coal, petroleum |

---

## Potential vs Developed Resources

| Potential | Developed |
|---|---|
| Found but not fully utilised | Surveyed and currently utilised |
| Future use possible | Current use |
| Example: potential solar energy in Rajasthan | Resources already being used |

---

## Power Sharing vs Federalism

| Power Sharing | Federalism |
|---|---|
| Broad concept of sharing power | Specific system of government |
| Can occur in many forms | Power divided among levels |
| Includes sharing among organs/groups/levels | Mainly division between levels |

---

## Income vs Non-Income Goals

| Income Goals | Non-Income Goals |
|---|---|
| Higher wages | Security |
| More employment | Respect |
| Higher profits | Freedom |
| Better income | Equality |
| More purchasing power | Education/health |
