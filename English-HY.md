# 1. A Letter to God

### Author: G.L. Fuentes

## 📝 Summary

Lencho was a poor farmer who lived with his family in a small house on the top of a hill. He depended completely on his crop for his livelihood.

One day, he saw dark clouds and expected rain. Initially, the rain made him happy because his crops needed water. However, the rain soon turned into a **hailstorm** and destroyed the entire crop.

Lencho became very sad because his family would have nothing to eat.

Lencho had complete faith in God. He wrote a letter to God asking for **100 pesos** to survive and sow his field again.

The postmaster read the letter. He was impressed by Lencho's faith and decided to help him. He collected money from his employees and friends and sent Lencho **70 pesos** in an envelope.

When Lencho received the money, he was not surprised because he completely trusted God. However, he became angry because he had received only 70 pesos.

He believed that the **post office employees had stolen the remaining 30 pesos**.

## ⭐ Important Points

- Lencho was a hardworking farmer.
    
- His crop was destroyed by a hailstorm.
    
- He had complete faith in God.
    
- He asked God for 100 pesos.
    
- The postmaster collected 70 pesos for him.
    
- Lencho thought the post office employees had stolen the remaining money.
    

## 🎯 Themes

- Faith and belief
    
- Human kindness
    
- Irony
    
- Innocence
    

## 📌 Irony

The greatest irony is that the people who actually helped Lencho were the same people whom he accused of stealing his money.

# 2. Nelson Mandela: Long Walk to Freedom

### Author: Nelson Mandela

## 📝 Summary

The chapter describes the inauguration of **Nelson Mandela as the first Black President of South Africa** on 10 May 1994.

The ceremony took place at the Union Buildings in Pretoria.

Mandela remembered the long struggle against **apartheid**, a system of racial discrimination in South Africa.

He honoured the people who had sacrificed their lives for freedom.

Mandela believed that courage was not the absence of fear but the **triumph over fear**.

He also explained that both the oppressed and the oppressor lose their humanity when freedom is denied.

## ⭐ Important Points

- Mandela became South Africa's first Black president.
    
- Apartheid was a system of racial discrimination.
    
- Mandela spent many years fighting for equality.
    
- He believed courage means overcoming fear.
    
- Freedom is necessary for every human being.
    
- Mandela wanted South Africa to become a democratic and equal society.
    

## 🎯 Themes

- Freedom
    
- Equality
    
- Courage
    
- Racial discrimination
    
- Human dignity
    

## ⭐ Important Quote Idea

**Courage is the victory over fear, not the absence of fear.**

# 3. Two Stories About Flying

## Part I — His First Flight

### Author: Liam O'Flaherty

### 📝 Summary

A young seagull was afraid to fly.

His brothers and sister had already learned to fly, but he lacked confidence.

His parents encouraged him, but he remained on the ledge.

One day, his mother came near him with a piece of fish but stopped just out of his reach.

Hungry, the young seagull jumped towards the fish.

He suddenly discovered that his wings could support him.

He began flying and became confident.

### ⭐ Theme

- Overcoming fear
    
- Self-confidence
    
- Courage
    
- Learning through experience
    

### 📌 Important Point

The young seagull learned that **fear can be overcome by taking the first step**.

# Part II — The Black Aeroplane

### Author: Frederick Forsyth

### 📝 Summary

A pilot was flying his old Dakota aeroplane from Paris to England.

He wanted to reach home quickly.

He saw a huge storm ahead but decided to fly through it.

Inside the storm, his compass and other instruments stopped working.

His fuel was also running low.

Suddenly, he saw another mysterious black aeroplane.

The unknown pilot signalled him to follow.

The Dakota pilot followed the black aeroplane and eventually reached the runway safely.

However, when he asked the control room about the other plane, the woman replied that **no other plane was visible on the radar**.

The story ends mysteriously.

## 🎯 Themes

- Mystery
    
- Fear
    
- Hope
    
- Supernatural possibility
    

## ❓ Mystery

The identity of the black aeroplane and its pilot is never explained.

# 4. From the Diary of Anne Frank

### Author: Anne Frank

## 📝 Summary

Anne Frank was a young Jewish girl who received a diary on her 13th birthday.

She named her diary **Kitty** and treated it as a close friend.

Anne felt that she could share her deepest thoughts with Kitty because she did not have a true friend with whom she could discuss everything.

She described her school life, classmates, teachers and her relationship with her family.

Her mathematics teacher, **Mr Keesing**, often punished her for talking too much in class.

He asked her to write essays about the habit of talking.

Anne wrote humorous essays and eventually convinced Mr Keesing that talking was a natural characteristic.

## ⭐ Important Characters

### Anne Frank

- Intelligent
    
- Honest
    
- Sensitive
    
- Talkative
    
- Observant
    
- Humorous
    

### Mr Keesing

- Anne's mathematics teacher
    
- Strict but ultimately understanding
    
- Punished Anne for talking
    

## 🎯 Themes

- Loneliness
    
- Friendship
    
- School life
    
- Self-expression
    
- Adolescence
    

## 📌 Important Point

Anne considered her diary **Kitty** because she wanted someone she could trust and talk to freely.

### Author: James Herriot

## 📝 Summary

Mrs Pumphrey's dog, **Tricki**, was extremely fat and unhealthy because she overfed him.

She gave him too many sweets and rich foods and did not give him enough exercise.

Dr Herriot realised that Tricki needed a strict diet and exercise.

He took Tricki to his surgery.

There, Tricki was given simple food and allowed to play with other dogs.

Within a short time, Tricki became active and healthy.

Mrs Pumphrey was extremely happy and believed that Dr Herriot had performed a great "surgery".

### Author: Robert Arthur

## 📝 Summary

# PART C — GRAMMAR

# 1. ACTIVE AND PASSIVE VOICE

## 🔹 Active Voice

The subject performs the action.

**Example:**

> Ram writes a letter.

Subject = Ram  
Verb = writes  
Object = a letter

## 🔹 Passive Voice

The subject receives the action.

> A letter is written by Ram.

---

## ⭐ Basic Formula

### Active:

**Subject + Verb + Object**

### Passive:

**Object + helping verb + V3 + by + Subject**

---

## 🔥 Tense Conversion Table

|Tense|Active|Passive|
|---|---|---|
|Simple Present|writes|is/am/are written|
|Present Continuous|is writing|is/am/are being written|
|Present Perfect|has written|has/have been written|
|Simple Past|wrote|was/were written|
|Past Continuous|was writing|was/were being written|
|Past Perfect|had written|had been written|
|Simple Future|will write|will be written|
|Future Perfect|will have written|will have been written|

### Example

Active:

> She writes a letter.

Passive:

> A letter is written by her.

Active:

> She wrote a letter.

Passive:

> A letter was written by her.

Active:

> She is writing a letter.

Passive:

> A letter is being written by her.

---

## ⚠️ Important

Only sentences having an **object** can normally be changed into passive voice.

Example:

> He sleeps.

There is no object, so passive voice cannot normally be formed.

---

# 2. REPORTED SPEECH

Reported speech tells us what someone said without quoting their exact words.

### Direct Speech

> He said, "I am tired."

### Indirect Speech

> He said that he was tired.

---

# 🔄 Common Changes

## Pronouns

Pronouns change according to the speaker and listener.

## Tense Changes

|Direct|Reported|
|---|---|
|is/am|was|
|are|were|
|was/were|had been|
|has/have|had|
|do/does|did|
|did|had done|
|will|would|
|can|could|
|may|might|

### Example

Direct:

> Rahul said, "I am busy."

Reported:

> Rahul said that he was busy.

---

# 🕐 Time & Place Changes

|Direct|Reported|
|---|---|
|today|that day|
|tomorrow|the next day|
|yesterday|the previous day|
|now|then|
|here|there|
|this|that|
|these|those|
|ago|before|

---

# ❓ Questions

### Direct:

> He said to me, "Where do you live?"

### Reported:

> He asked me where I lived.

### Yes/No Question

Direct:

> He said, "Are you ready?"

Reported:

> He asked me if I was ready.

---

# 📢 Commands / Requests

### Direct:

> Teacher said to the students, "Open your books."

### Reported:

> The teacher ordered the students to open their books.

### Request

> He said to me, "Please help me."

→ He requested me to help him.

---

# 💡 Useful Reporting Verbs

|Situation|Verb|
|---|---|
|Statement|said / told|
|Question|asked|
|Order|ordered|
|Request|requested|
|Advice|advised|
|Suggestion|suggested|
|Exclamation|exclaimed|

---

# 3. MODALS

Modals are helping verbs that express ability, possibility, permission, necessity, obligation, advice, etc.

## Important Modals

|Modal|Use|
|---|---|
|can|ability / permission|
|could|past ability / polite request|
|may|permission / possibility|
|might|weaker possibility|
|must|strong necessity|
|should|advice|
|would|polite request / preference|
|will|future / willingness|
|shall|suggestion / future|
|ought to|duty / advice|
|need|necessity|
|used to|past habit|

---

## Examples

### Can

> I can swim.

### Could

> Could you help me?

### May

> May I come in?

### Might

> It might rain today.

### Must

> You must obey the rules.

### Should

> You should study regularly.

### Would

> Would you like some tea?

### Ought to

> We ought to respect our elders.

---

# ⭐ Must vs Should

**Must** = strong necessity

> You must wear a helmet.

**Should** = advice

> You should exercise regularly.

---

# 4. PREPOSITIONS

Prepositions show the relationship between words.

Examples:

**in, on, at, by, with, from, to, for, since, between, among, under, over, into, through**

---

## 📍 Time

### At

Used for exact time.

> at 5 PM  
> at noon  
> at night

### On

Used for days and dates.

> on Monday  
> on 15 August

### In

Used for months, years and longer periods.

> in January  
> in 2026  
> in the morning

---

## 📍 Place

### In

Something is inside an area/place.

> He lives in Delhi.

### On

Something is touching a surface.

> The book is on the table.

### At

A particular point/place.

> He is at school.

---

## ⭐ Since vs For

### Since

Starting point of time.

> I have lived here since 2020.

### For

Duration of time.

> I have lived here for six years.

---

## ⭐ Between vs Among

### Between

Usually used for two.

> Divide the money between Rahul and Aman.

### Among

Used for more than two.

> She distributed sweets among the children.

# PART D — WRITING SKILLS

# 1. FORMAL LETTER

Formal letters are written to:

- Principal
    
- Editor
    
- Government officer
    
- Municipal authority
    
- Company/organisation
    
- Other officials
    

---

# ✉️ FORMAL LETTER FORMAT

**Sender's Address**

**Date**

**Receiver's Designation/Address**

**Subject: __________**

**Sir/Madam,**

**Paragraph 1 – Introduction**

State the purpose of writing the letter.

**Paragraph 2 – Details**

Explain the problem, situation or information clearly.

**Paragraph 3 – Request/Conclusion**

State what action you expect and end politely.

**Yours faithfully,**

**Name**

---

## 📝 Example Structure

> 23, ABC Colony  
> Lucknow  
> 29 September 2026
> 
> The Editor  
> The Times of India  
> Lucknow
> 
> **Subject: Need for better road safety**
> 
> Sir/Madam,
> 
> I am writing to draw attention to...
> 
> Firstly,...
> 
> Moreover,...
> 
> I request the concerned authorities to...
> 
> Yours faithfully,  
> XYZ

---

# ⭐ Formal Letter Rules

✅ Use formal language  
✅ Keep the subject short and clear  
✅ Divide the letter into paragraphs  
✅ State the purpose clearly  
✅ Use polite language  
❌ Avoid slang  
❌ Avoid emojis  
❌ Avoid informal expressions

---

# 2. APPLICATION TO THE PRINCIPAL

An application is generally written to request something officially.

Examples:

- Leave
    
- Fee concession
    
- Change of subject
    
- Permission
    
- Certificate
    
- School facility
    

---

# 📝 APPLICATION FORMAT

**Sender's Address / Class & Section**

**Date**

**The Principal**

**School Name**

**School Address**

**Subject: __________**

**Respected Sir/Madam,**

### Paragraph 1

Introduce yourself and state the purpose.

### Paragraph 2

Explain the reason/details.

### Paragraph 3

Make the request politely.

**Thank you.**

**Yours obediently,**

**Name:** ______  
**Class:** X  
**Roll No.:** ______

---

# 📌 FORMAL LETTER vs APPLICATION

|Formal Letter|Application|
|---|---|
|Can be written to many authorities|Usually written to an authority/Principal|
|May be about public/social issues|Usually requests permission/action|
|Often includes detailed issue|Usually focuses on a specific request|
|Ends with Yours faithfully|Often ends with Yours obediently|

# ⭐ EXAM TIP

For literature answers:

**Point → Explanation → Evidence/Event → Conclusion**

Don't simply retell the whole story.

For a 3–5 mark answer, write **2–3 clear paragraphs** with relevant events and the central idea.

For writing questions, **format + content + formal language** are all important.
