# Quadratic Equations

## 📖 Overview

This chapter studies equations where the highest power of the variable is 2. It covers solving by factorisation and by the quadratic formula, plus the nature of roots.

## 🧠 Key Concepts

- **Quadratic equation:** An equation of the form:

$$ax^2+bx+c=0,\quad a\ne 0$$

- **Root of an equation:** A value of `x` that satisfies the equation.
- **Solving by factorisation:** Split the middle term or factor the expression, then use the zero product rule.
- **Quadratic formula:** Used when factorisation is difficult.
- **Discriminant:** The expression $$b^2-4ac$$. It tells the nature of roots.
- **Real roots only:** The current Class 10 syllabus focuses on real roots.

## 📐 Formulas

For:

$$ax^2+bx+c=0$$

Quadratic formula:

$$x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$$

Discriminant:

$$D=b^2-4ac$$

Nature of roots:

| Value of $$D$$ | Nature of roots |
|---|---|
| $$D>0$$ | Two distinct real roots |
| $$D=0$$ | Two equal real roots |
| $$D<0$$ | No real roots |

## ✏️ Examples

**Example 1:** Solve:

$$x^2-5x+6=0$$

$$x^2-2x-3x+6=0$$

$$(x-2)(x-3)=0$$

So, $$x=2$$ or $$x=3$$.

**Example 2:** Find the nature of roots of:

$$2x^2-4x+3=0$$

$$D=(-4)^2-4(2)(3)=16-24=-8$$

Since $$D<0$$, there are no real roots.

## 💡 Important Points

- Always write the equation in standard form before identifying `a`, `b`, and `c`.
- Factorisation works only when factors are easy to find.
- The discriminant is used only to discuss nature of roots.
- If $$D=0$$, the root is $$-\frac{b}{2a}$$.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Forgetting the $$\pm$$ sign in the quadratic formula.
- Taking `a` as 0, which would not be a quadratic equation.
- Calculating $$b^2-4ac$$ with wrong signs.

## 📝 Exam Tips

- For word problems, first form the equation carefully.
- Check roots in the original equation when possible.
- Use the quadratic formula neatly; small sign errors are common.

## ⚡ Quick Revision

- Standard form: $$ax^2+bx+c=0$$.
- Formula: $$x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$$.
- Discriminant decides the nature of real roots.
