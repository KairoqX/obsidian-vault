# Some Applications of Trigonometry

## 📖 Overview

This chapter uses trigonometric ratios to find heights and distances in real-life situations.

## 🧠 Key Concepts

- **Line of sight:** The line from the observer's eye to the object.
- **Angle of elevation:** The angle made when the object is above the horizontal line.
- **Angle of depression:** The angle made when the object is below the horizontal line.
- **Right-triangle model:** Most problems are solved by converting the situation into one or two right triangles.
- **Syllabus limit:** Problems should not involve more than two right triangles, and angles are usually $$30^\circ,45^\circ,60^\circ$$.

## 📐 Formulas

The main ratios used are:

$$\tan\theta=\frac{\text{height}}{\text{horizontal distance}}$$

$$\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$$

$$\cos\theta=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$$

Useful values:

$$\tan30^\circ=\frac{1}{\sqrt3},\quad \tan45^\circ=1,\quad \tan60^\circ=\sqrt3$$

## ✏️ Examples

**Example 1:** A pole casts a shadow of 10 m when the angle of elevation of the sun is $$45^\circ$$. Find the pole's height.

$$\tan45^\circ=\frac{h}{10}$$

$$1=\frac{h}{10}\Rightarrow h=10\text{ m}$$

**Example 2:** From a point 20 m away from a tower, the angle of elevation is $$60^\circ$$.

$$\tan60^\circ=\frac{h}{20}$$

$$h=20\sqrt3\text{ m}$$

## 💡 Important Points

- Angle of elevation and angle of depression are measured from the horizontal.
- Heights are vertical; distances on ground are horizontal.
- A rough diagram is very important.
- Use the eye level of the observer when it is mentioned.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Confusing angle of elevation with angle of depression.
- Using sine or cosine when tangent is simpler.
- Ignoring observer height in tower/building problems.
- Forgetting units in final answer.

## 📝 Exam Tips

- Always draw a labelled diagram.
- Identify the right triangle before writing any ratio.
- Use exact values like $$\sqrt3$$ unless decimal approximation is asked.

## ⚡ Quick Revision

- Elevation: object above horizontal.
- Depression: object below horizontal.
- Most height-distance questions use tangent.
- Standard angles: $$30^\circ,45^\circ,60^\circ$$.
