# Coordinate Geometry

## 📖 Overview

This chapter connects algebra and geometry by placing points on the coordinate plane. The syllabus focuses on distance formula and section formula.

## 🧠 Key Concepts

- **Coordinate plane:** A plane with x-axis and y-axis.
- **Point:** Written as $$(x,y)$$.
- **Distance between two points:** Found using the distance formula.
- **Section formula:** Used to find a point that divides a line segment in a given ratio.
- **Midpoint:** A special case of section formula where the ratio is $$1:1$$.

## 📐 Formulas

Distance between $$A(x_1,y_1)$$ and $$B(x_2,y_2)$$:

$$AB=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}$$

Section formula for internal division in ratio $$m:n$$:

$$\left(\frac{mx_2+nx_1}{m+n},\frac{my_2+ny_1}{m+n}\right)$$

Midpoint formula:

$$\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\frac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)$$

## ✏️ Examples

**Example 1:** Find the distance between $$(1,2)$$ and $$(4,6)$$.

$$d=\sqrt{(4-1)^2+(6-2)^2}=\sqrt{9+16}=5$$

**Example 2:** Find the midpoint of $$(2,3)$$ and $$(6,7)$$.

$$\left(\frac{2+6}{2},\frac{3+7}{2}\right)=(4,5)$$

## 💡 Important Points

- Distance is always non-negative.
- The order of points does not change the distance.
- In section formula, carefully match the ratio with the correct endpoint.
- Midpoint means equal division.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Forgetting the square root in the distance formula.
- Writing coordinates in the wrong order.
- Using external division formula; the current syllabus specifies internal division.

## 📝 Exam Tips

- Draw a quick rough graph when the question is geometry-based.
- Label points before substituting values.
- Keep signs carefully, especially with negative coordinates.

## ⚡ Quick Revision

- Point: $$(x,y)$$.
- Distance formula uses squared differences.
- Section formula finds a point dividing a segment internally.
- Midpoint is section formula with ratio $$1:1$$.
