# Arithmetic Progressions

## 📖 Overview

This chapter studies number patterns where the difference between consecutive terms is constant.

## 🧠 Key Concepts

- **Arithmetic Progression (AP):** A list of numbers in which each term after the first is obtained by adding a fixed number.
- **First term:** Usually denoted by `a`.
- **Common difference:** Usually denoted by `d`.

$$d=a_2-a_1=a_3-a_2=\cdots$$

- **nth term:** The general term of an AP.
- **Sum of first n terms:** Total of the first `n` terms of an AP.
- **Finite AP:** An AP with a fixed number of terms.

## 📐 Formulas

nth term:

$$a_n=a+(n-1)d$$

Sum of first `n` terms:

$$S_n=\frac{n}{2}[2a+(n-1)d]$$

If last term is `l`:

$$S_n=\frac{n}{2}(a+l)$$

Common difference:

$$d=a_{n+1}-a_n$$

## ✏️ Examples

**Example 1:** Find the 10th term of 3, 7, 11, 15, ...

Here, $$a=3,\ d=4,\ n=10$$.

$$a_{10}=3+(10-1)4=39$$

**Example 2:** Find the sum of first 20 terms of 5, 8, 11, ...

$$a=5,\ d=3,\ n=20$$

$$S_{20}=\frac{20}{2}[2(5)+(20-1)3]=10(67)=670$$

## 💡 Important Points

- A sequence is an AP only if the common difference is constant.
- The common difference can be positive, negative, or zero.
- Word problems often ask for `n`, `a_n`, or `S_n`.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Using $$a_n=a+nd$$ instead of $$a_n=a+(n-1)d$$.
- Mixing the term number `n` with the term value.
- Forgetting that `d` may be negative.

## 📝 Exam Tips

- First identify `a`, `d`, and `n`.
- Use the last-term sum formula when the last term is given.
- In word problems, translate "first", "next", "total", and "number of terms" carefully.

## ⚡ Quick Revision

- AP means constant difference.
