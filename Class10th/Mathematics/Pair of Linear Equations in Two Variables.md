# Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables

## 📖 Overview

This chapter teaches how to solve two linear equations together. It is important for word problems where two unknown quantities are related.

## 🧠 Key Concepts

- **Linear equation in two variables:** An equation of the form:

$$ax+by+c=0$$

where `a`, `b`, and `c` are real numbers and `a` and `b` are not both zero.
- **Solution of a pair:** A value of `x` and `y` that satisfies both equations.
- **Graphical method:** Draw both lines. Their point of intersection gives the solution.
- **Consistent pair:** The equations have at least one solution.
- **Inconsistent pair:** The equations have no solution.
- **Dependent pair:** The equations represent the same line and have infinitely many solutions.
- **Algebraic methods in syllabus:** substitution and elimination.

## 📐 Formulas

For the pair:

$$a_1x+b_1y+c_1=0$$

$$a_2x+b_2y+c_2=0$$

Conditions:

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| $$\frac{a_1}{a_2}\ne\frac{b_1}{b_2}$$ | One unique solution |
| $$\frac{a_1}{a_2}=\frac{b_1}{b_2}\ne\frac{c_1}{c_2}$$ | No solution |
| $$\frac{a_1}{a_2}=\frac{b_1}{b_2}=\frac{c_1}{c_2}$$ | Infinitely many solutions |

## ✏️ Examples

**Example 1:** Solve:

$$x+y=7$$

$$x-y=1$$

Add both equations:

$$2x=8\Rightarrow x=4$$

Then:

$$4+y=7\Rightarrow y=3$$

Solution: $$(4,3)$$

**Example 2:** Check the nature of:

$$2x+3y-6=0,\quad 4x+6y-12=0$$

$$\frac{2}{4}=\frac{3}{6}=\frac{-6}{-12}$$

So, infinitely many solutions.

## 💡 Important Points

- Intersecting lines have one solution.
- Parallel lines have no solution.
- Coincident lines have infinitely many solutions.
- Word problems usually need careful choice of variables first.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Comparing $$c_1/c_2$$ with the wrong sign.
- Forgetting to substitute back after finding one variable.
- Taking only one equation's solution as the final answer.

## 📝 Exam Tips

- Write "Let the two variables be..." clearly in word problems.
- Use elimination when coefficients can be made equal easily.
- Use substitution when one equation already gives one variable simply.

## ⚡ Quick Revision

- Two linear equations represent two straight lines.
- Solution = intersection point.
- Use ratio conditions to identify number of solutions.
- Main algebraic methods: substitution and elimination.
