# Circles

## 📖 Overview

This chapter studies tangents to a circle and their important properties.

## 🧠 Key Concepts

- **Circle:** Set of all points at a fixed distance from a fixed point.
- **Radius:** The fixed distance from the centre to a point on the circle.
- **Tangent:** A line that touches a circle at exactly one point.
- **Point of contact:** The point where a tangent touches the circle.
- **Secant:** A line that intersects a circle at two points.
- **Main theorem 1:** The tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.
- **Main theorem 2:** Tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal in length.

## 📐 Formulas

If `P` is an external point and `PA`, `PB` are tangents to a circle:

$$PA=PB$$

If `OA` is radius and `PA` is tangent at `A`:

$$OA\perp PA$$

So:

$$\angle OAP=90^\circ$$

## ✏️ Examples

**Example 1:** From point `P`, two tangents `PA` and `PB` are drawn to a circle. If $$PA=7\text{ cm}$$, then:

$$PB=7\text{ cm}$$

**Example 2:** If radius `OA` is drawn to tangent `PA`, then:

$$\angle OAP=90^\circ$$

This often helps form a right triangle.

## 💡 Important Points

- A tangent touches the circle at one point only.
- Radius to the point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent.
- Equal tangents theorem applies only from the same external point.
- Tangent problems often use [[Triangles]] and Pythagoras theorem.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Treating a secant as a tangent.
- Forgetting to mark the right angle between radius and tangent.
- Assuming tangents from different external points are equal.

## 📝 Exam Tips

- Draw the radius to the point of contact.
- Look for right triangles after drawing the radius.
- Use equal tangents clearly with a reason.

## ⚡ Quick Revision

- Tangent touches circle once.
- Radius is perpendicular to tangent at contact point.
- Tangents from the same external point are equal.
