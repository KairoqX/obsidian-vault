# Carbon and its Compounds

## 📖 Overview

This chapter explains why carbon forms so many compounds and introduces hydrocarbons, functional groups, soaps, detergents, and important carbon reactions.

## 🧠 Key Concepts

- Carbon has valency 4, so it forms four covalent bonds.
- Covalent bonds are formed by sharing electrons.
- Catenation is the ability of carbon to form long chains, branches, and rings.
- Saturated hydrocarbons have only single bonds.
- Unsaturated hydrocarbons have double or triple bonds.
- Homologous series is a family of organic compounds with the same functional group and similar chemical properties.
- Functional groups decide many chemical properties of carbon compounds.
- Ethanol and ethanoic acid are important carbon compounds in the syllabus.
- Soaps and detergents help remove oily dirt by forming micelles.

## 📌 Important Points

- Carbon forms covalent compounds because it cannot easily lose or gain four electrons.
- Alkanes have single bonds.
- Alkenes have at least one carbon-carbon double bond.
- Alkynes have at least one carbon-carbon triple bond.
- Addition reactions occur in unsaturated hydrocarbons.
- Substitution reactions are common in saturated hydrocarbons.

## 📐 Formulas

General formulae:

$$C_nH_{2n+2}$$ for alkanes

$$C_nH_{2n}$$ for alkenes

$$C_nH_{2n-2}$$ for alkynes

Important compounds:

$$CH_3CH_2OH$$ = ethanol

$$CH_3COOH$$ = ethanoic acid

## 🧪 Important Reactions

Combustion:

$$CH_4 + 2O_2 \rightarrow CO_2 + 2H_2O + \text{heat and light}$$

Oxidation of ethanol:

$$CH_3CH_2OH + 2[O] \rightarrow CH_3COOH + H_2O$$

Addition reaction:

$$CH_2=CH_2 + H_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Ni}} CH_3-CH_3$$

Substitution reaction:

$$CH_4 + Cl_2 \xrightarrow{\text{sunlight}} CH_3Cl + HCl$$

Ethanol with sodium:

$$2C_2H_5OH + 2Na \rightarrow 2C_2H_5ONa + H_2$$

Ethanoic acid with sodium carbonate:

$$2CH_3COOH + Na_2CO_3 \rightarrow 2CH_3COONa + H_2O + CO_2$$

Esterification:

$$CH_3COOH + C_2H_5OH \xrightarrow{\text{conc. }H_2SO_4} CH_3COOC_2H_5 + H_2O$$

Saponification:

$$\text{Ester} + NaOH \rightarrow \text{Sodium salt of acid} + \text{Alcohol}$$

## ✏️ Examples

- Methane is a saturated hydrocarbon.
- Ethene is an unsaturated hydrocarbon.
- Ethanoic acid reacts with carbonates to release carbon dioxide.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Confusing saturated and unsaturated hydrocarbons.
- Forgetting carbon has valency 4.
- Writing ionic bonds for carbon compounds.
- Mixing up ethanol and ethanoic acid.

## 📝 Exam Tips

- Learn functional groups with examples.
- For reactions, write conditions like nickel catalyst, sunlight, or concentrated sulphuric acid where needed.
