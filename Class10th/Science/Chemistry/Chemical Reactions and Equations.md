# Chemical Reactions and Equations

## 📖 Overview

This chapter explains how substances change during chemical reactions and how those reactions are written as balanced chemical equations.

## 🧠 Key Concepts

- A chemical reaction forms one or more new substances.
- Signs of a reaction include change in colour, change in state, gas formation, temperature change, or precipitate formation.
- A chemical equation uses symbols and formulae to represent reactants and products.
- A balanced equation has the same number of atoms of each element on both sides.
- Main types of reactions:
  - Combination reaction
  - Decomposition reaction
  - Displacement reaction
  - Double displacement reaction
  - Oxidation and reduction
- Exothermic reactions release heat.
- Endothermic reactions absorb heat.
- Corrosion is the slow damage of metals due to reaction with air, water, or chemicals.
- Rancidity is oxidation of fats and oils that changes smell and taste.

## 📌 Important Points

- Balancing equations follows the law of conservation of mass.
- A precipitate is an insoluble solid formed in a solution.
- Oxidation means gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen.
- Reduction means loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen.
- Antioxidants and airtight packing help slow rancidity.

## 🧪 Important Reactions

Combination:

$$2Mg + O_2 \rightarrow 2MgO$$

$$CaO + H_2O \rightarrow Ca(OH)_2 + \text{heat}$$

Decomposition:

$$CaCO_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} CaO + CO_2$$

$$2Pb(NO_3)_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2PbO + 4NO_2 + O_2$$

$$2AgCl \xrightarrow{\text{sunlight}} 2Ag + Cl_2$$

Displacement:

$$Fe + CuSO_4 \rightarrow FeSO_4 + Cu$$

Double displacement:

$$BaCl_2 + Na_2SO_4 \rightarrow BaSO_4\downarrow + 2NaCl$$

Redox:

$$CuO + H_2 \rightarrow Cu + H_2O$$

## 🖼️ Diagrams / Visuals

- Learn to show reactants on the left and products on the right in a balanced chemical equation.
- For precipitation reactions, show formation of an insoluble solid using a downward arrow.

## ✏️ Examples

- Burning of magnesium ribbon forms white magnesium oxide.
- Iron nails kept in copper sulphate solution displace copper and form iron sulphate.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Changing chemical formulae while balancing equations.
- Forgetting state symbols when the question asks for them.
- Confusing oxidation with reduction.
- Writing unbalanced equations in final answers.

## 📝 Exam Tips

- First write the correct skeleton equation, then balance atoms.
- Balance hydrogen and oxygen after other elements if the equation is complex.
- Mention conditions such as heat or sunlight when they are important.

## ⚡ Quick Revision

- Reactants are written on the left; products on the right.
- Balanced equation = equal atoms of every element on both sides.
- Combination: two or more substances form one product.
- Decomposition: one substance breaks into simpler substances.
- Displacement: a more reactive element displaces a less reactive one.
- Redox includes oxidation and reduction together.
