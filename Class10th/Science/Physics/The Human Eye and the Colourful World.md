# The Human Eye and the Colourful World

## 📖 Overview

This chapter explains the human eye, defects of vision, and natural optical phenomena such as dispersion and scattering.

## 🧠 Key Concepts

- The human eye forms images on the retina.
- The eye lens changes focal length to focus on near and distant objects.
- Accommodation is the ability of the eye lens to adjust its focal length.
- Near point of a normal eye is about 25 cm.
- Far point of a normal eye is infinity.
- Myopia means difficulty seeing distant objects clearly.
- Hypermetropia means difficulty seeing nearby objects clearly.
- Presbyopia is age-related weakening of accommodation.
- Dispersion is splitting of white light into colours.
- Scattering of light explains blue sky and reddish sunrise or sunset.

## 📌 Important Points

- Myopia is corrected using a concave lens.
- Hypermetropia is corrected using a convex lens.
- The prism splits white light into a spectrum.
- Red light bends least and violet bends most in a prism.
- Atmospheric refraction causes twinkling of stars and advance sunrise.

## ✏️ Examples

- Stars twinkle because of atmospheric refraction.
- The sky appears blue due to scattering of shorter wavelengths.
- The sun appears reddish near sunrise and sunset because longer red light reaches the eye more easily.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Confusing myopia and hypermetropia corrections.
- Saying planets twinkle exactly like stars; planets usually do not twinkle noticeably.
- Forgetting the order of colours in the spectrum.
- Mixing dispersion with scattering.

## 📝 Exam Tips

- Learn eye-defect diagrams with correct lens type.
- Explain natural phenomena using keywords: refraction, dispersion, scattering.
- Remember: myopia uses concave lens; hypermetropia uses convex lens.
