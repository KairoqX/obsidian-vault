# Electricity

## 📖 Overview

This chapter studies electric current, potential difference, resistance, Ohm's law, combinations of resistors, heating effect, and electric power.

## 🧠 Key Concepts

- Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge.
- Potential difference is work done per unit charge.
- Resistance opposes the flow of current.
- Ohm's law states that current through a conductor is directly proportional to potential difference across it, if temperature remains constant.
- Resistivity is a material property.
- Resistors can be connected in series or parallel.
- Heating effect of current is used in electric heaters and fuses.
- Electric power is the rate of electrical energy consumption.

## 📌 Important Points

- SI unit of current is ampere (A).
- SI unit of potential difference is volt (V).
- SI unit of resistance is ohm ($$\Omega$$).
- Ammeter is connected in series.
- Voltmeter is connected in parallel.
- In series, current is same through all resistors.
- In parallel, potential difference is same across all branches.

## 📐 Formulas

Electric current:

$$I=\frac{Q}{t}$$

Potential difference:

$$V=\frac{W}{Q}$$

Ohm's law:

$$V=IR$$

Resistance of a conductor:

$$R=\rho\frac{l}{A}$$

Series resistance:

$$R_s=R_1+R_2+R_3+\cdots$$

Parallel resistance:

$$\frac{1}{R_p}=\frac{1}{R_1}+\frac{1}{R_2}+\frac{1}{R_3}+\cdots$$

Heating effect:

$$H=I^2Rt$$

Electric power:

$$P=VI$$

$$P=I^2R$$

$$P=\frac{V^2}{R}$$

Electrical energy:

$$E=Pt$$

Commercial unit:

$$1\text{ kWh}=3.6\times10^6\text{ J}$$

## ✏️ Examples

**Example 1:** If $$Q=20\text{ C}$$ flows in $$5\text{ s}$$:

$$I=\frac{Q}{t}=\frac{20}{5}=4\text{ A}$$

**Example 2:** If $$V=12\text{ V}$$ and $$R=6\Omega$$:

$$I=\frac{V}{R}=2\text{ A}$$

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Connecting voltmeter in series.
- Forgetting unit conversion for kWh and joules.
- Using series formula for parallel circuits.
- Confusing resistance and resistivity.

## 📝 Exam Tips

- Draw circuit diagrams with correct symbols.
- Write units after every numerical answer.
- For resistor combinations, first identify series or parallel.
- Use $$P=VI$$, $$P=I^2R$$, or $$P=\frac{V^2}{R}$$ according to given data.

## ⚡ Quick Revision

- Current: $$I=\frac{Q}{t}$$.
- Ohm's law: $$V=IR$$.
- Series resistance adds directly.
- Parallel resistance adds reciprocally.
- Heat produced: $$H=I^2Rt$$.
- Power: $$P=VI$$.
