# Light - Reflection and Refraction

## 📖 Overview

This chapter studies how light reflects from mirrors and refracts through lenses and transparent materials.

## 🧠 Key Concepts

- Reflection is bouncing back of light from a surface.
- Refraction is bending of light when it passes from one medium to another.
- Spherical mirrors are concave or convex.
- Lenses are convex or concave.
- Principal axis, pole, focus, centre of curvature, and focal length are important terms.
- Real images can be obtained on a screen.
- Virtual images cannot be obtained on a screen.
- Refractive index compares the speed of light in two media.
- Lens power tells how strongly a lens converges or diverges light.

## 📌 Important Points

- Concave mirror can form real or virtual images depending on object position.
- Convex mirror always forms virtual, erect, diminished images.
- Convex lens can form real or virtual images.
- Concave lens always forms virtual, erect, diminished images.
- Light bends towards the normal when it enters a denser medium from a rarer medium.
- Light bends away from the normal when it enters a rarer medium from a denser medium.

## 📐 Formulas

Mirror formula:

$$\frac{1}{f}=\frac{1}{v}+\frac{1}{u}$$

Lens formula:

$$\frac{1}{f}=\frac{1}{v}-\frac{1}{u}$$

Magnification for mirror:

$$m=-\frac{v}{u}=\frac{h'}{h}$$

Magnification for lens:

$$m=\frac{v}{u}=\frac{h'}{h}$$

Refractive index:

$$n=\frac{c}{v}$$

Power of lens:

$$P=\frac{1}{f}$$

where `f` is in metre and unit of power is dioptre (D).

## ✏️ Examples

- Rear-view mirrors in vehicles are convex mirrors.
- A magnifying glass uses a convex lens.
- A concave mirror is used by dentists to see enlarged images.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Mixing mirror and lens sign conventions.
- Forgetting to convert focal length from centimetres to metres for power.
- Confusing real and virtual image properties.
- Drawing rays without arrow directions.
