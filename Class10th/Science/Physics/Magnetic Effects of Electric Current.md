# Magnetic Effects of Electric Current

## 📖 Overview

This chapter explains the magnetic field produced by electric current and the working ideas behind electromagnets, electric motors, and domestic electric circuits.

## 🧠 Key Concepts

- A current-carrying conductor produces a magnetic field.
- Magnetic field lines show the direction and strength of a magnetic field.
- Right-hand thumb rule gives the direction of magnetic field around a current-carrying conductor.
- A current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a force.
- Fleming's left-hand rule gives the direction of force.
- Solenoid behaves like a bar magnet when current passes through it.
- Electromagnetic induction produces induced current when magnetic field linked with a conductor changes.
- Fleming's right-hand rule gives direction of induced current.
- Domestic circuits use live wire, neutral wire, earth wire, fuse, and switches.

## 📌 Important Points

- Magnetic field lines never intersect.
- Field lines are closer where magnetic field is stronger.
- The magnetic field inside a current-carrying solenoid is nearly uniform.
- Fuse wire protects circuits by melting when current is too high.
- Earthing protects users from electric shock.
- Overloading and short-circuiting are common causes of electrical hazards.

## ✏️ Examples

- An electromagnet is made by passing current through a coil wound around soft iron.
- Electric bells use electromagnets.
- A fuse melts when excess current flows through it.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Confusing Fleming's left-hand rule with right-hand rule.
- Drawing intersecting magnetic field lines.
- Forgetting that current direction affects magnetic field direction.
- Confusing live, neutral, and earth wires.

## 📝 Exam Tips

- Practise hand rules using labelled fingers.
- Draw magnetic field lines with arrows.
- In domestic circuit questions, mention fuse and earthing clearly.

## ⚡ Quick Revision

- Current produces magnetic field.
- Right-hand thumb rule: magnetic field around conductor.
- Fleming's left-hand rule: force on current-carrying conductor.
- Fleming's right-hand rule: induced current.
- Fuse prevents damage due to excess current.
- Earthing prevents electric shock.
