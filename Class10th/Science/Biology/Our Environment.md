# Our Environment

## 📖 Overview

This chapter explains ecosystems, food chains, energy flow, ozone layer depletion, and waste management.

## 🧠 Key Concepts

- Environment includes living and non-living surroundings.
- Ecosystem is a system formed by organisms interacting with each other and with physical surroundings.
- Producers make food, usually by photosynthesis.
- Consumers depend on other organisms for food.
- Decomposers break down dead organisms and recycle nutrients.
- Food chain shows transfer of food and energy.
- Food web is a network of connected food chains.
- Trophic level is the feeding level in a food chain.
- Biological magnification is the increase of harmful chemicals at higher trophic levels.
- Ozone layer protects Earth from harmful ultraviolet radiation.
- Waste can be biodegradable or non-biodegradable.

## 📌 Important Points

- Energy flow in an ecosystem is unidirectional.
- Only a small part of energy passes to the next trophic level.
- Non-biodegradable substances persist in the environment for a long time.
- Decomposers are important for nutrient recycling.
- CFCs damage the ozone layer.

## 📐 Formulas

Ten percent law idea:

$$\text{Energy transferred to next trophic level}\approx 10\%$$

## 🖼️ Diagrams / Visuals

- Food chain diagram: shows producer to top consumer.
- Food web diagram: shows interconnected food chains.
- Ozone layer diagram: shows protection from ultraviolet rays.

## ✏️ Examples

- Grass → deer → tiger is a food chain.
- Plastic is non-biodegradable.
- Vegetable peels are biodegradable.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Thinking energy cycles like nutrients.
- Confusing food chain with food web.
- Forgetting that producers form the first trophic level.
- Saying biodegradable waste never causes pollution.

## 📝 Exam Tips

- Label trophic levels clearly.
- Use arrows in food chains to show direction of energy flow.
- Learn one example each of biodegradable and non-biodegradable waste.
