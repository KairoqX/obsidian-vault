# Heredity

## 📖 Overview

This chapter explains how traits are passed from parents to offspring and how Mendel's work helped explain inheritance.

## 🧠 Key Concepts

- Heredity is the transfer of traits from one generation to the next.
- Variation means differences among individuals of the same species.
- Traits are controlled by genes.
- Genes are located on chromosomes.
- DNA carries genetic information.
- Dominant trait shows even when only one copy is present.
- Recessive trait shows only when both copies are recessive.
- Mendel studied inheritance using pea plants.
- Monohybrid cross studies one pair of contrasting traits.
- Sex determination in humans depends on sex chromosomes.

## 📌 Important Points

- Humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes.
- Females have XX sex chromosomes.
- Males have XY sex chromosomes.
- The father determines the sex of the child in humans because sperm may carry X or Y chromosome.
- In Mendel's monohybrid cross, the F2 phenotypic ratio is commonly 3:1.
- Acquired traits are not usually inherited because they do not change germ-cell DNA.

## 🖼️ Diagrams / Visuals

- Monohybrid cross: shows inheritance of one contrasting trait.
- Sex determination chart: shows XX and XY combinations.
- Simple chromosome and gene diagram: shows genes located on chromosomes.

## ✏️ Examples

- Tallness and dwarfness in pea plants are contrasting traits.
- If a sperm carrying X fertilises the egg, the child is female.
- If a sperm carrying Y fertilises the egg, the child is male.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Confusing dominant with more common.
- Saying the mother determines the sex of the child.
- Mixing inherited traits with acquired traits.
- Forgetting that genes occur in pairs for a trait.

## 📝 Exam Tips

- Draw Punnett squares neatly.
- Use clear symbols for dominant and recessive alleles.
- Explain sex determination using X and Y chromosomes.

## ⚡ Quick Revision

- Heredity passes traits from parents to offspring.
- Genes control traits.
- Dominant traits express with one copy.
- Recessive traits need two copies.
- Human females are XX; males are XY.
- Father determines the sex of the child.
