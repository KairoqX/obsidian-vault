# Control and Coordination

## 📖 Overview

This chapter explains how organisms respond to changes using the nervous system, hormones, and plant movements.

## 🧠 Key Concepts

- Control and coordination help organisms respond properly to stimuli.
- A stimulus is a change in the environment.
- Receptors detect stimuli.
- Neurons carry messages as electrical impulses.
- A reflex action is a quick, automatic response.
- The brain and spinal cord form the central nervous system.
- Hormones are chemical messengers released by endocrine glands.
- Plants coordinate using hormones and growth movements.
- Tropic movements are directional growth responses in plants.

## 📌 Important Points

- Reflex arc includes receptor, sensory neuron, spinal cord, motor neuron, and effector.
- Brain parts in syllabus include forebrain, midbrain, hindbrain, cerebellum, medulla, and pons.
- Auxin helps in plant growth responses.
- Adrenaline prepares the body for emergency situations.
- Insulin regulates blood sugar level.
- Iodine is needed for thyroxine production.

## ✏️ Examples

- Pulling your hand away from a hot object is a reflex action.
- Plant shoot bends towards light due to phototropism.
- Roots grow towards gravity due to geotropism.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

- Confusing nerves with hormones.
- Thinking reflex actions always need conscious thinking.
- Mixing up phototropism and geotropism.
- Forgetting that plant responses are usually slower than animal responses.

## 📝 Exam Tips

- Draw reflex arc neatly and label arrows.
- Learn one function of each major endocrine gland.
- Use examples when explaining tropic movements.

## ⚡ Quick Revision

- Nervous control is fast and electrical.
- Hormonal control is slower and chemical.
- Reflex action protects the body quickly.
- Auxin controls many plant growth movements.
- Insulin controls blood sugar level.
