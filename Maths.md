# 1. REAL NUMBERS

## 🔹 Euclid's Division Lemma

For positive integers **a** and **b**:

### ⭐

**a = bq + r**

where:

- a = dividend
    
- b = divisor
    
- q = quotient
    
- r = remainder
    
- **0 ≤ r < b**
    

---

# 🔢 Euclid's Division Algorithm

Used to find the **HCF** of two positive integers.

### Steps:

1. Divide the larger number by the smaller number.
    
2. Use the remainder as the new divisor.
    
3. Continue until the remainder becomes 0.
    
4. The last non-zero remainder is the **HCF**.
    

---

# ⭐ Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Every composite number can be expressed as a product of primes, and this factorisation is **unique**, apart from the order of the factors.

### Example:

**60 = 2² × 3 × 5**

---

# 🔗 HCF AND LCM

If two positive integers are **a** and **b**:

### ⭐

**HCF × LCM = a × b**

---

# 🔢 Irrational Numbers

Numbers that cannot be expressed in the form:

**p/q**

where p and q are integers and **q ≠ 0**.

Examples:

**√2, √3, √5, π**

---

# 📌 Decimal Expansion

For a rational number **p/q**, where p and q are coprime:

### Terminating decimal

If the prime factorisation of q is of the form:

**2ᵐ × 5ⁿ**

then the decimal expansion terminates.

### Non-terminating recurring decimal

If q contains any prime factor other than **2 or 5**, the decimal expansion is non-terminating recurring.

### Example:

**3/8**

8 = 2³

→ terminating

**7/12**

12 = 2² × 3

→ non-terminating recurring

---

# 🧠 QUICK REVISION

**Euclid → a = bq + r**

**HCF × LCM = product of numbers**

**Only 2 and 5 in denominator → terminating decimal**

# 2. POLYNOMIALS

A polynomial in x is an expression such as:

**ax² + bx + c**

where a, b and c are constants.

---

# 🔹 Degree of Polynomial

Highest power of the variable.

### Examples:

**5x + 2** → degree 1

**x² + 3x + 2** → degree 2

**2x³ + x** → degree 3

---

# 🔹 Types

### Linear Polynomial

Degree = 1

**ax + b**

### Quadratic Polynomial

Degree = 2

**ax² + bx + c**

### Cubic Polynomial

Degree = 3

**ax³ + bx² + cx + d**

---

# ⭐ Zero of a Polynomial

A number **k** is a zero of polynomial p(x) if:

### **p(k) = 0**

---

# 🔢 Relation Between Zeroes and Coefficients

For:

### **ax² + bx + c**

If zeroes are **α and β**:

### ⭐

**α + β = -b/a**

### ⭐

**αβ = c/a**

---

# For Cubic Polynomial

For:

**ax³ + bx² + cx + d**

If zeroes are α, β and γ:

### ⭐

**α + β + γ = -b/a**

**αβ + βγ + γα = c/a**

**αβγ = -d/a**

---

# 🧠 QUICK REVISION

For **ax² + bx + c**:

**Sum of zeroes = -b/a**

**Product of zeroes = c/a**

---

# 3. PAIR OF LINEAR EQUATIONS IN TWO VARIABLES

General form:

### ⭐

**a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0**

**a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0**

---

# 📈 Graphical Meaning

The two equations represent two straight lines.

### 1. Intersecting Lines

One solution.

**a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂**

---

### 2. Parallel Lines

No solution.

**a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂**

---

### 3. Coincident Lines

Infinitely many solutions.

**a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂**

---

# 🧮 Methods of Solving

## 1. Graphical Method

Draw both lines.

The point where they intersect is the solution.

---

## 2. Substitution Method

1. Make one variable the subject.
    
2. Substitute it into the other equation.
    
3. Solve for the remaining variable.
    
4. Substitute back.
    

---

## 3. Elimination Method

1. Make coefficients of one variable equal.
    
2. Add or subtract equations.
    
3. Find one variable.
    
4. Substitute to find the other.
    

---

## 4. Cross-Multiplication Method

For:

**a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0**

**a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0**

### ⭐

**x/(b₁c₂ − b₂c₁)**

=

**y/(c₁a₂ − c₂a₁)**

=

**1/(a₁b₂ − a₂b₁)**

---

# 🧠 QUICK REVISION

**Intersecting → one solution**

**Parallel → no solution**

**Coincident → infinitely many solutions**

---

# 4. QUADRATIC EQUATIONS

A quadratic equation is an equation of the form:

### ⭐

**ax² + bx + c = 0**

where:

**a ≠ 0**

---

# 🧮 Methods of Solving

## 1. Factorisation

Split the middle term and factorise.

Example:

**x² + 5x + 6 = 0**

**(x + 2)(x + 3) = 0**

Therefore:

**x = -2, -3**

---

# 2. Completing the Square

Convert the equation into a perfect square form.

---

# 3. Quadratic Formula

### ⭐ MOST IMPORTANT

**x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac))/2a**

---

# 🔥 Discriminant

### ⭐

**D = b² - 4ac**

The discriminant tells us the nature of roots.

---

# 📊 Nature of Roots

### D > 0

Two distinct real roots.

### D = 0

Two equal real roots.

### D < 0

No real roots.

---

# 🔢 Relationship Between Roots

For:

**ax² + bx + c = 0**

If roots are α and β:

### ⭐

**α + β = -b/a**

### ⭐

**αβ = c/a**

---

# 🧠 QUICK REVISION

**Quadratic → ax² + bx + c = 0**

**Formula → (-b ± √(b²−4ac))/2a**

**D = b²−4ac**

**D > 0 → distinct roots**

**D = 0 → equal roots**

**D < 0 → no real roots**

---

# 5. ARITHMETIC PROGRESSIONS

An arithmetic progression (AP) is a sequence in which the difference between consecutive terms is constant.

### Example:

**2, 5, 8, 11, 14...**

Common difference:

**d = 3**

---

# 🔹 General AP

**a, a+d, a+2d, a+3d...**

Where:

**a = first term**

**d = common difference**

---

# ⭐ nth TERM

### ⭐

**aₙ = a + (n−1)d**

---

# 🔢 Sum of First n Terms

### ⭐

**Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n−1)d]**

---

# Alternative Formula

If the last term is **l**:

### ⭐

**Sₙ = n/2 (a + l)**

---

# 🔹 Finding Common Difference

**d = a₂ − a₁**

or

**d = aₙ − aₙ₋₁**

---

# 🧠 IMPORTANT AP FORMULAS

**nth term → aₙ = a + (n−1)d**

**Sum → Sₙ = n/2[2a+(n−1)d]**

**Sum using last term → Sₙ = n/2(a+l)**

---

# 6. COORDINATE GEOMETRY

Coordinate geometry deals with points and their positions on the Cartesian plane.

A point is written as:

### **(x, y)**

---

# 📍 Distance Formula

For points:

**A(x₁,y₁)** and **B(x₂,y₂)**

### ⭐

**AB = √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²]**

---

# 🧮 Section Formula

If a point P divides the line segment joining:

**A(x₁,y₁)** and **B(x₂,y₂)**

in the ratio **m:n**, then:

### ⭐

**P = ((mx₂ + nx₁)/(m+n), (my₂ + ny₁)/(m+n))**

---

# 🔹 Midpoint Formula

For points A(x₁,y₁) and B(x₂,y₂):

### ⭐

**Midpoint = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2)**

---

# 📐 Area of Triangle

For vertices:

**(x₁,y₁), (x₂,y₂), (x₃,y₃)**

### ⭐

**Area = 1/2 |x₁(y₂−y₃) + x₂(y₃−y₁) + x₃(y₁−y₂)|**

---

# 🔥 Important Condition

If area of triangle = **0**:

The three points are **collinear**.

---

# 🧠 QUICK REVISION

**Distance → √[(x₂−x₁)²+(y₂−y₁)²]**

**Midpoint → ((x₁+x₂)/2,(y₁+y₂)/2)**

**Section → weighted average of coordinates**

**Area = 0 → collinear points**

---

# 7. TRIANGLES

## 🔺 Similar Figures

Two figures are similar if they have the same shape but not necessarily the same size.

For similar triangles:

- Corresponding angles are equal.
    
- Corresponding sides are proportional.
    

---

# ⭐ Criteria for Similarity

## 1. AAA

If corresponding angles are equal, triangles are similar.

---

## 2. SSS

If corresponding sides are proportional, triangles are similar.

---

## 3. SAS

If one pair of corresponding angles is equal and the sides including those angles are proportional, triangles are similar.

---

# 📐 Basic Proportionality Theorem

If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle and intersects the other two sides, then it divides those two sides in the same ratio.

### If DE ∥ BC:

**AD/DB = AE/EC**

---

# 🔄 Converse of BPT

If a line divides two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then it is parallel to the third side.

---

# ⭐ Areas of Similar Triangles

The ratio of areas of two similar triangles is equal to the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides.

### ⭐

**Area₁/Area₂ = (Side₁/Side₂)²**

---

# 📐 Pythagoras Theorem

In a right-angled triangle:

### ⭐

**Hypotenuse² = Base² + Perpendicular²**

or

**c² = a² + b²**

---

# 🔄 Converse of Pythagoras Theorem

If:

**c² = a² + b²**

then the triangle is right-angled opposite the side c.

---

# 📌 IMPORTANT TRIANGLE RESULTS

If:

**ΔABC ~ ΔDEF**

Then:

### Corresponding angles:

**∠A = ∠D**

**∠B = ∠E**

**∠C = ∠F**

### Corresponding sides:

**AB/DE = BC/EF = AC/DF**

---

# 🧠 TRIANGLES QUICK REVISION

**AAA → Similar**

**SSS → Similar**

**SAS → Similar**

**BPT → Parallel line divides sides proportionally**

**Similar triangle areas → square of side ratio**

**Pythagoras → c² = a²+b²**
