# 1. Communication Skills – II

## Communication

**Communication** is the process of exchanging information, ideas, thoughts, feelings or messages between two or more people.

### Elements of Communication

1. **Sender** — Person who sends the message.
2. **Message** — Information being communicated.
3. **Encoding** — Converting an idea into a message.
4. **Channel/Medium** — Method used to send the message.
5. **Receiver** — Person who receives the message.
6. **Decoding** — Understanding the received message.
7. **Feedback** — Response given by the receiver.

### Types of Communication

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Verbal** | Communication using words | Speaking, writing |
| **Non-verbal** | Communication without words | Gestures, facial expressions, posture |
| **Visual** | Communication using visual elements | Charts, signs, symbols |

## Active Listening

**Active listening** means listening carefully, understanding the speaker's message and responding appropriately.

### Good Listening Habits

- Pay attention to the speaker.
- Avoid interrupting.
- Maintain appropriate eye contact.
- Ask questions when necessary.
- Give feedback.
- Avoid distractions.

## Barriers to Communication

A **communication barrier** is anything that prevents a message from being communicated or understood properly.

### Examples

- Language barrier
- Noise
- Lack of attention
- Poor listening
- Cultural differences
- Emotional barriers
- Lack of clarity

## 7 Cs of Effective Communication

1. **Clear** — Easy to understand.
2. **Concise** — Short and to the point.
3. **Concrete** — Specific and definite.
4. **Correct** — Free from errors.
5. **Coherent** — Logical and connected.
6. **Complete** — Contains all necessary information.
7. **Courteous** — Polite and respectful.

# 2. Self-Management Skills – II

## Self-Management

**Self-management** is the ability to manage one's thoughts, emotions, behaviour, time and activities effectively.

### Important Self-Management Skills

- Self-motivation
- Time management
- Stress management
- Goal setting
- Self-awareness
- Self-control

## Self-Motivation

**Self-motivation** is the ability to motivate yourself to achieve your goals without depending completely on others.

### Benefits of Self-Motivation

- Helps achieve goals.
- Builds confidence.
- Improves productivity.
- Helps overcome difficulties.
- Encourages positive thinking.

---

## Goal Setting

**Goal setting** means deciding what you want to achieve and planning how to achieve it.

### SMART Goals

> **S — Specific**  
> **M — Measurable**  
> **A — Achievable**  
> **R — Realistic / Relevant**  
> **T — Time-bound**

### Example

> "I will complete two chapters of Science by Sunday."

This is better than:

> "I will study more."

because it clearly specifies **what**, **how much** and **when**.

---

## Time Management

**Time management** means planning and organising your time so that tasks can be completed effectively.

### Ways to Manage Time

- Make a timetable.
- Prioritise important tasks.
- Set deadlines.
- Break large tasks into smaller tasks.
- Avoid procrastination.
- Avoid unnecessary distractions.
- Review your progress.

## Procrastination

**Procrastination** means unnecessarily delaying or postponing a task.

### Example

> You have homework due tomorrow but keep watching videos instead of doing it.

---

## Stress Management

**Stress** is a feeling of mental or emotional pressure caused by difficult situations or demands.

### Ways to Manage Stress

- Exercise regularly.
- Get enough sleep.
- Take short breaks.
- Manage your time.
- Practise relaxation techniques.
- Talk to someone you trust.
- Maintain a positive attitude.

# 3. ICT Skills – II

## ICT

**ICT** stands for **Information and Communication Technology**.

ICT refers to technologies used to **create, store, process, retrieve and communicate information**.

### Examples of ICT

- Computers
- Smartphones
- Internet
- Email
- Video conferencing
- Cloud storage
- Digital communication platforms

---

## Hardware

**Hardware** refers to the physical components of a computer that can be seen and touched.

### Examples

- Keyboard
- Mouse
- Monitor
- CPU
- Printer
- Hard disk

## Software

**Software** is a set of programs or instructions that tells a computer what to do.

### Examples

- Windows
- Linux
- LibreOffice
- Web browsers
- Media players

### Hardware vs Software

| Hardware | Software |
|---|---|
| Physical components | Programs/instructions |
| Can be touched | Cannot be physically touched |
| Requires software to perform useful tasks | Requires hardware to run |
| Keyboard, mouse, monitor | Windows, LibreOffice, Chrome |

---

## Operating System

An **Operating System (OS)** is system software that manages computer hardware and provides an interface between the user and the computer.

### Examples

- Windows
- Linux
- macOS
- Android
- iOS

### Functions of an Operating System

- Manages hardware.
- Manages files and folders.
- Manages memory.
- Manages applications.
- Provides a user interface.
- Controls input and output devices.

---

## Internet Safety

Internet safety means protecting yourself, your data and your devices while using the internet.

### Important Safety Practices

- Use strong passwords.
- Use different passwords for important accounts.
- Never share your password or OTP.
- Don't click suspicious links.
- Don't download files from untrusted sources.
- Keep software updated.
- Use antivirus/security software.
- Avoid sharing personal information with strangers.
- Use secure websites.

### Strong Password

A strong password should contain a combination of:

- Uppercase letters
- Lowercase letters
- Numbers
- Special characters

Example:

`R@h7#kP92!`

> [!warning] Never use someone else's example password as your real password.

# 4. Entrepreneurial Skills – II

## Entrepreneur

An **entrepreneur** is a person who identifies a business opportunity, starts and manages a business and takes calculated risks to achieve goals.

## Entrepreneurship

**Entrepreneurship** is the process of identifying opportunities and creating, organising and managing a business.

---

## Qualities of an Entrepreneur

### 1. Creativity

Ability to generate new ideas and solutions.

### 2. Innovation

Ability to introduce new or improved products, services or methods.

### 3. Risk-taking

An entrepreneur should be willing to take **calculated risks**.

### 4. Decision-making

Ability to make suitable decisions at the right time.

### 5. Leadership

Ability to guide and motivate others.

### 6. Self-confidence

Belief in one's own abilities and decisions.

### 7. Persistence

Ability to continue working despite difficulties or failure.

### 8. Problem-solving

Ability to identify problems and find suitable solutions.

---

## Innovation

**Innovation** means introducing a new idea, product, service or method, or improving an existing one.

### Example

A company develops a faster and more efficient version of an existing product.

---

## Business Opportunity

A **business opportunity** is a situation or idea that can be developed into a successful business.

### How to Identify Opportunities

- Identify people's needs.
- Find problems that need solutions.
- Observe market trends.
- Study customer requirements.
- Look for gaps in existing products or services.
- Think of new and useful ideas.

---

## Risk in Entrepreneurship

Entrepreneurs face uncertainty and risks while starting and running a business.

### Calculated Risk

A **calculated risk** is a risk taken after analysing its possible advantages, disadvantages and consequences.

> An entrepreneur should not take unnecessary risks. They should analyse the situation before making a decision.
